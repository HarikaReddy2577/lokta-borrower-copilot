BORROWER COPILOT - RULES (plain English, "if this then that")
================================================================

1. AFFORDABILITY (how much of income can safely go to loan payments)
   - Salaried, stable income: existing EMIs + new EMI should not pass 50% of net
     income for what a LENDER would likely approve, but only 35-40% for what is
     SAFE for the borrower to carry.
   - Self-employed with ITR (tax return) proof: lender cap 40%, safe cap 30%.
   - Self-employed cash-only / informal / gig work: lender cap 30% (lenders are
     stricter when income can't be verified), safe cap 25%.
   - Why two numbers: a lender approves based on their own risk appetite; the
     safe number leaves the borrower room for emergencies. Always tell the
     borrower to use the SAFE number, not the lender number.

2. UNKNOWN CREDIT SCORE
   - Never treat "I don't know my score" as a bad score. Never assume 300.
   - Instead, apply a middle-of-the-road interest rate (not the best, not the
     worst) and say plainly: "Your rate could be better if you check and share
     your actual score."

3. INTEREST RATE BAND
   - Personal loan, credit score 750+: 10.5% - 13%
   - Personal loan, credit score 650-749: 13% - 17%
   - Personal loan, score unknown or below 650: 17% - 22%
   - Loan against property or gold (secured): 9% - 12%, regardless of credit
     score, because the asset lowers the lender's risk.
   - Always also show the ALL-IN cost: rate + processing fee (usually 1-2% of
     the loan amount) spread over the loan term, so it's a fair number to
     compare against what a lender quotes.

4. WHEN TO ROUTE TO A SECURED LOAN INSTEAD OF A PERSONAL LOAN
   - If the borrower owns property, gold, or another asset worth at least 2x
     the loan amount, AND has no formal credit score or has informal income,
     recommend a secured product (loan against property/gold) instead of a
     personal loan.
   - Reason: unsecured lenders approve very little (or nothing) for
     unverifiable income, and the rates would be punishing.

5. WHEN THE VERDICT SHOULD BE "DON'T BORROW" OR "BORROW LESS"
   - If the borrower already has high-cost debt (roughly 25%+ interest) AND has
     missed a payment in the last year: verdict is "Don't borrow more" -
     recommend addressing existing debt first.
   - If the new EMI would push total obligations past the SAFE cap in Rule 1:
     verdict is "Borrow less," and show the amount that does fit.
   - If a household earner recently lost income and there is no savings
     cushion: lean toward "Don't borrow" unless the loan is for something
     essential.

6. EMI CEILING AND STRESS TEST
   - EMI ceiling = (net income - normal household expenses - existing EMIs)
     x the safe cap percentage from Rule 1.
   - Always also show a stress case: recompute the ceiling assuming income
     drops 20% or the interest rate rises 2 percentage points, and say
     whether the loan still fits under that stress.

7. CONFIDENCE / RANGES
   - The fewer questions the borrower answers, the wider every range shown
     must be. Never narrow a range unless a specific answer justifies it.
   - Always explain a number in one sentence, e.g.: "Your EMI ceiling is
     Rs 22,000 because your safe cap is 35% of your Rs 1,10,000 income after
     your existing Rs 14,000 car EMI and rent."
